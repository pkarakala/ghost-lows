# Design decisions

The dated log of design decisions (D1–D19), amendments (A1–A13) and proposals (P1–P9); for reviewers checking what was fixed before which result was seen, and for anyone proposing a change (AGENTS.md rule 11).

Decisions on the open items in [`SPEC_REVIEW.md`](../development/SPEC_REVIEW.md), drafted and recorded on 2026-10-18 by an AI agent acting in the PI-reviewer role defined in `CLAUDE.md`, under authority delegated by the project lead, and accepted by the project lead. Each one gives the evidence, the decision and the spec it changes. The specs (`RESEARCH.md`, `SIMULATION.md`, `BENCHMARK.md`) are the source of truth; this file records why. Decisions on the research question (D2, D3, D5, D6) are frozen with the protocol tag `protocol-v1` before the full benchmark runs (Phase 6). Changes after that are logged here as amendments, with the reason.

## Evidence: pilot of stock simglucose

`experiments/pilot/stock_simglucose.py` with `analyse_stock.py`: stock basal-bolus controller, `RandomScenario`, 5-min sensor, all 30 virtual patients, 14 days, seed 1. Truth = plasma glucose. An event = ≥15 min below the threshold.

| Finding | Value |
|---|---|
| Level-2 events (plasma <54) | 144 in total: children 119, adolescents 14, adults 11 (10 of them in one adult) |
| Patients with no level-2 event / none at night | 13 / 19 of 30 |
| Implausible lows | plasma nadir below 25 mg/dL in 4 patients (5.6, 6.4, 17.1 and 21.1 mg/dL). Two runs stopped early because simglucose ends a run when glucose falls below 10. Nights spent below 70: 84%, 79% and 73% in three children |
| Bolus IOB (4-h linear) at nocturnal level-1 onsets vs. random night times | AUC 0.54 (81 onsets) |
| Lag: simglucose's CGM signal (Gsub) reaching 70 after plasma onset | median 15 min (IQR 10–20) |
| Share of the night with plasma in 70–95 mg/dL (a median-depth PISA there gives a CGM <70) | median 28% (patients range 0–80%) |

Also verified in the simglucose 0.2.11 source: the stock controller adds the full correction bolus on *every* 5-minute step in which the patient is eating, not once per meal. The model has no glucagon state and no hypoglycaemia-dependent insulin action. The subcutaneous compartment (x[12]) feeds nothing back into the model.

**Reading.** The stock cohort is not a credible population. Level-2 sensitivity would be estimated mostly on a few virtual children with chronic, implausibly deep nocturnal hypoglycaemia caused by their therapy settings. The premise that "context reads the insulin that causes every low" does not hold at night (AUC 0.54). The simulated sensor lag sits right at the edge of the lows-caught window.

## Decisions

| ID | Decision | Resolves | Spec |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | **Truth = plasma glucose** (x[3]/Vg) at 1-min resolution; simglucose's Gsub is kept as `isf_glucose`. The CGM is built from Gsub (the model's own lag, τ = 1/ksc) plus noise, with no extra filter. **Sensitivity arm:** CGM re-derived from plasma with τ = 6 min [Basu 2013]. This is valid because x[12] does not feed back. | SR1 | SIM §1 |
| D2 | **Factorial context ablation:** CGM-only, +time of day, +IOB/COB, +both. Within each detector, all arms pass through the same learned combiner and differ only in their inputs. The "as published" rule is reported as a reference. **Primary RQ2 contrast:** (CGM+time+IOB/COB) vs. (CGM+time). | SR13 | RES §2, BENCH §2 |
| D3 | **Leak axis:** three scenario mixes, *standard*, *default* and *high*. The realised leak, AUC(IOB at nocturnal true-low onsets vs. random night times), is reported for each mix and for real data (AZT1D, user-entered boluses only). | SR14 | SIM §3, BENCH §4 |
| D4 | **Urgent-low floor:** every alarm includes an ungated alarm on any CGM reading ≤55 mg/dL [assumption; CR14], mirroring the non-silenceable device alarm. No gate or context can suppress it. Floor alarms and late announcements are reported separately. | SR21 | BENCH §1, §4 |
| D5 | **Operating point:** one scalar score per alarm. Gated alarms tune only the gate. θ\* minimises false alarms/day on training patients subject to level-2 sensitivity (floor included) ≥ the CGM-only trend alarm's on the same patients. δ = 0 is primary; δ = 0.05 is a sensitivity analysis. Ties go to the more sensitive θ. At least 10 level-2 events per training fold are required, else the fold is flagged. Test-fold sensitivity and threshold-free curves are always reported. | SR20 | BENCH §5 |
| D6 | **Hypotheses:** H1 is a slope contrast (trend − threshold). H2 is a rate ratio with a smallest effect size of interest of RR = 0.75 and pre-set reading rules [assumption; CR9], relative because absolute rates scale with the assumed PISA frequency. H3 is tested on semi-synthetic AZT1D nights. | SR23 | RES §3 |
| D7 | **Retrospective detectors** run in real time as named causal adaptations (`*_causal`), with every deviation documented and detection latency reported. The retrospective originals are scored on the retrospective track. | SR6 | BENCH §2 |
| D8 | **Generator-mismatch arm:** test PISAs come from perturbed shapes (τ ×0.5 and ×2; depth quantiles ×0.75 and ×1.25) while detectors keep the nominal model. | SR15 | SIM §4.4 |
| D9 | **Target population for RQ2:** open-loop insulin users who record boluses and carbs. There is no automated insulin delivery in the core benchmark, which keeps post-hoc fault injection valid. Real AID data uses user-entered boluses and carbs only. Correction to SR16: a PISA still running at breakfast (onsets from 05:00) can lower the CGM used for the breakfast correction, so such overlaps are counted and reported. | SR16 | RES §1, SIM §2, §4.5 |
| D10 | **Cause attribution at the alarm**, in order: PISA if one was active in [t−30, t] min; other fault in [t−30, t]; *near-event* if within 60 min after a true low ends or 30–60 min before an onset; otherwise noise. Windows configurable. | SR17 | BENCH §4 |
| D11 | **Events:** truth on 1-min plasma, ≥15 consecutive minutes below the threshold, ending after ≥15 consecutive minutes at or above it [Battelino 2023]. Real data: a run of CGM readings below the threshold spanning ≥15 min, with a gap of more than two sampling intervals ending the run. Level 2 is nested in level 1; each level is scored separately; false-alarm matching uses level-1 events. | SR18 | BENCH §3 |
| D12 | **Matching:** an alarm matches the first event with t ∈ [onset−30 min, end]. An event is caught if an alarm falls in [onset−30, onset+15]. Alarms in (onset+15, end] are late, not false. Window sensitivity: early 20/30/45 min, late 15/30 min. | SR19 | BENCH §4 |
| D13 | **Units and severity:** cause-attributed counts per night equal counts per 24 h of wear (one night per day). Severity is the fraction of nights with ≥1 PISA, with one PISA per compressed night [assumption]. Severity never changes PISA depth or duration. | SR22 | SIM §4.1, §4.3 |
| D14 | **Statistics:** results by age group and pooled. Patient bootstrap stratified by age group (2,000 resamples, percentile intervals), described as conditional on the simulator cohort. Estimation framing outside the H1–H3 rules. Reporting follows ADEMP [Morris 2019]. Feature normalisation uses training folds or trailing windows only, with a test. | SR24 | BENCH §6 |
| D15 | **Drop the "82% from noise" figure** (unverifiable); keep the rationale. | SR25 | RES §2 |
| D16 | **Simulator validity in hypoglycaemia:** the 2008 model lacks the 2013 hypoglycaemia nonlinearity and glucagon [Dalla Man 2014]; the paper gives the equations but not r1, r2 or the glucagon parameter values, so it cannot be added faithfully. Stated as the primary limitation. The **semi-synthetic real-data track becomes co-primary for RQ1**, and simulated low shapes get a face-validity check against real CGM. | new | RES §6–7, SIM §5 |
| D17 | **Controller rewrite and calibration:** one bolus per meal from announced carbs and one correction per meal. Each patient's basal and carb-ratio scale is calibrated on the *standard* scenario, on calibration seeds kept apart from benchmark seeds, so that 14-day TBR <70 is within 1–8% and the plasma nadir stays ≥40 mg/dL [assumption; CR15]. Lows deeper than that are outside the 2008 model's credible range. The calibrated cohort is compared with real per-person TBR (DiaData). *Not done as of 2026-10-18: DiaData is not used.* | new | SIM §2 |
| D18 | **Publication, citation, usability:** a versioned benchmark definition (`ghostlows-bench v1`); the frozen simulated cohort released as a citable dataset; `CITATION.cff`; a DOI per release; a CLI and a plug-in alarm API with a tested example; a results page; a JOSS software paper and a JDST methods paper. | new | ROADMAP |
| D19 | **Fit once, freeze, then sweep.** For each fold and scenario mix, learned alarms are fitted and every threshold chosen on training patients at one *training* fault level: the realistic default PISA fraction (Lobo 2026's rate reproduced on the default-mix cohort, capped at every night) with other faults moderate. The frozen alarms are then evaluated at every sweep level. RQ1 asks how faults degrade *existing* alarms, and a deployed alarm is not re-tuned for each fault rate; re-fitting per level would also cost six times as much. Models are fitted per mix so that each mix is its own world for the leak axis (D3). | — | BENCH §5 |

## Amendments

Each entry gives the date, whether any full-benchmark result existed at the time, the change, and the reason. The tag `protocol-v1` is never moved.

- **A1 (2026-10-18; no full-cohort result yet). The gated alarms now follow D5 as written.** At the tag the pipeline applied the D5 operating point only to the combiner arms and `gbm`. Gated alarms kept a fixed threshold of 0 on the score 1[trend] × (1 − gate). The binary Baysal gate then behaved as published, but the continuous matched-filter and isolation-forest gates almost never reach 1, so they never suppressed an alarm. This was found in the semi-synthetic Weinstock run, where both gates scored exactly like `trend`. A gate selected without its combiner arms was also never fitted, so the robustness configs would have failed. Now every gated alarm is fitted, and D5 chooses its gate threshold on training patients (BENCHMARK.md §5). The trend alarm's own rule, the hypotheses, the estimands and the configs are unchanged.
- **A2 (2026-10-18; no full-cohort result yet). Finer candidate thresholds in the upper tail.** D5 chooses θ\* from candidate thresholds. The code used 30 training-score quantiles spaced evenly from 0.5 to 0.999. That leaves no candidate between alarms firing on 1.8% of samples and on 0.1%, which is where low alarms operate (time below range is a few percent). Fifteen quantiles at log-spaced firing fractions from 5% to 0.01% are added. This brings the code closer to the rule as written, which minimises over θ. It applies equally to every learned alarm and to both arms of every H2 contrast. Found by reading the code, not from results.
- **A3 (2026-10-18; no full-cohort result yet). RQ3 is operationalised as an exploratory analysis.** RQ3 was in the protocol, but no estimand or comparator had been fixed. They are fixed now, before any RQ3 result exists (RESEARCH.md RQ3). `kalman_pisa` and two ablations are added as *stretch alarms*: configs select them by name, and `alarms: all` stays the pre-registered 30, so no H1/H2 output changes. "The best discriminative detector" is chosen per fold on training patients only, to avoid choosing the winner on test results. Disclosure: the filter's design was developed on the standard mix of the final cohort, including held-out results on one fold. Its headline is therefore read on the default mix, and RQ3 makes no confirmatory claim. Development also changed the design before any RQ3 run, for reasons recorded in BENCHMARK.md §2 (Phase 9 notes): a manoeuvre mode, a sensor-error state, and tuning by average precision instead of likelihood.
- **A4 (2026-10-18; no AZT1D result yet). The H3 reading.** H3 said the AZT1D rate ratio is "closer to 1 than in simulation at a matched unexplained-low fraction" but fixed neither the matching nor the reading. Matching: the simulated mix whose leak AUC (IOB at nocturnal low onsets vs. the night, D3) is closest to AZT1D's. Reading: per detector, at PISA on every night, H3 holds when |log RR| on AZT1D is smaller than on the matched simulated mix. Both ratios are reported with CIs. The reading is descriptive: the ratios come from independent samples, and real artifacts in the AZT1D reference are unlabelled. AZT1D context uses user-entered boluses and carbs only (D9). It runs on 5 leave-patients-out folds, with alarms fitted at PISA on half the nights as in the Weinstock track.
- **A5 (2026-10-18; before the real-data runs used for results). The real-data operating point matches level 1 sensitivity.** In the semi-synthetic real-data track the reference lows are taken from the CGM itself. The ungated floor (CGM ≤ 55) therefore catches every level 2 low (CGM < 54 for 15 min) by construction. The D5 constraint becomes trivial, and every learned alarm collapses to its fewest-alarms threshold: in a first Weinstock run, every alarm, including `floor_only`, caught 100% of level 2 lows. The real-data track therefore matches level 1 sensitivity to the trend alarm's. The simulation benchmark, whose truth is plasma glucose, keeps D5 at level 2.
- **A6 (2026-10-18; no benchmark result on the cohort). Virtual patients treat their lows.** The first full cohort (sim_full, 450 runs) was physiologically impossible in the perturbed mixes. Without treatment, time below 54 mg/dL was 0.0% (standard), 5.6% (default) and 20.6% (high); median run nadirs were 59, 22 and 0.5 mg/dL; and 147 of 450 runs went below 10 mg/dL. The 2008 model has no counter-regulation, and nobody treated the lows, so they lasted until the insulin wore off (median level 1 duration 267 min in the default mix). The engine now applies the 15-15 rule [CDC 2024] on the fault-free CGM: below 70 mg/dL by day, at or below 55 at night (SIMULATION.md §2, CR17). Rescue carbohydrate is not logged. A pilot (6 patients, 14 days, seed 1) gave 0.8 / 2.4 / 2.6% below 70 and 0.0 / 0.3 / 0.8% below 54, with mean nadirs 65 / 48 / 42 mg/dL. The default mix had 0.55 level 1 and 0.095 level 2 lows per day, close to AZT1D's CGM-referenced 0.51 and 0.10. The research questions, mixes, perturbation values and calibration are unchanged; the cohort is re-simulated. The untreated cohort is kept on disk as evidence but is not used.
- **A7 (2026-10-18; no benchmark result yet). D5 in folds with few level 2 lows.** D5 flags a training fold with fewer than 10 level 2 lows but named no threshold for it. The code then matched a NaN reference and fell back to the most sensitive candidate, which would make learned alarms fire constantly. With treatment, the standard mix has almost no level 2 lows. Such folds now match level 1 sensitivity instead and stay flagged (`match_level` and `estimable` in `thresholds.csv`). Folds with enough level 2 lows are unchanged.
- **A8 (2026-10-18; no benchmark result yet). H2 includes gbm.** H2 is "per detector", and gbm's arms are its feature sets (BENCHMARK.md §2). The contrast code paired only the combiner arms, so `gbm_time_ctx` over `gbm_time` was never computed. It now is. No other contrast changes.
- **A9 (2026-10-18; after all results). Correction to A3's disclosure.** A3 says the Kalman filter was developed "on the standard mix of the final cohort, including held-out results on one fold". The commit history shows more. After A3 was written (5c01d2f), a five-fold held-out check on the standard mix led to widening the filter's noise grid (sensor-error variance up to 900 (mg/dL)², time constant up to 90 min; 795752b). That check also scored the RQ3 comparators `gbm` and `gbm_ctx`, and it was not logged as an amendment. It ran on the untreated cohort that A6 later replaced, not on the final cohort. RQ3 stays exploratory, and its headline stays on the default mix, which no development run used. No result changes. Found in the repository review (`development/REVIEW.md`, M1).

- **A10 (2026-10-18; post hoc, after all v0.1.0 results were seen). Warning-time CIs resample patients (adopts P1).** `warning_times` passed one row per patient-run to the patient bootstrap, so a patient's five seeds were resampled as if they were five patients. This breaks D14 and `CLAUDE.md` hard rule 5, and the CIs are too narrow. Now each patient is reduced to one value, the median over its runs of each run's median warning (runs with at least one caught low), and the estimate is the median over patients, with the usual stratified patient bootstrap. A test fails if the unit is not the patient. *Expected to change:* the warning-time estimates and CIs in `warning.csv` for every benchmark config (point estimates too, since the median is now over patients, not runs), and the warning columns of `results/README.md`. Miss rates, sensitivities, false alarms and every contrast are unchanged. The v0.1.0 values stay in the tag `v0.1.0` and side by side with the new ones in `results/tables/amendments/A10__warning_old_vs_new.csv`.
- **A11 (2026-10-18; post hoc, after all v0.1.0 results were seen). Trade-off curves (adopts P2, as amended by the project lead).** D5 and BENCHMARK.md §4 require threshold-free curves; v0.1.0 reported only operating points, and at D5's δ = 0 the three as-published gates suppressed few or no trend alarms, so "the gates switch off" and the H2 null could not be told apart from "the zero-loss constraint forbids any suppression". Definition, fixed before computing:
  - *Alarms.* As published: `threshold`, `trend`, `baysal_gate`, `matched_filter_causal_gate`, `iforest_causal_gate`. With and without insulin and carb context: the H2 pair of each of the six detectors (`<detector>_combined_time_ctx` vs `<detector>_combined_time`; `gbm_time_ctx` vs `gbm_time`), and `kalman_pisa` vs `kalman_pisa_cgm` (stretch alarms, exploratory as in A3).
  - *Fitting.* As D19: per fold and mix, fitted on training patients at the realistic training level, then frozen. Candidate thresholds as in D5 (A2); for the two rule alarms, whose published threshold D5 never tunes, the candidates also include the published threshold, which is marked on the curve.
  - *Sweep.* For a retention r from 0.50 to 1.00 in steps of 0.01, the threshold in each fold and mix is the one D5 picks with the constraint "training level 2 sensitivity ≥ r × the trend alarm's" (fewest training false alarms per day; ties to the more sensitive). r = 1.00 is the pre-registered δ = 0 point. The points r = 0.95, 0.99 and 1.00 are marked. (D5's δ = 0.05 arm is an absolute margin; at the trend alarm's training sensitivity of about 0.9 it lies near r = 0.94.)
  - *Evaluation.* Each fold's thresholds are applied to its held-out patients at PISA on every night with other faults moderate (the level at which H2 is read). Level 2 sensitivity and false alarms per day from compression and from other causes are pooled over test patients, with 95% CIs from the stratified patient bootstrap (2,000 resamples).
  - *Readings (secondary, labelled so).* (i) For each gate and mix, the largest r below 1 at which compression false alarms per day are lower than at r = 1 with a paired-bootstrap CI of the ratio below 1, and the cost there: the paired difference in test level 2 sensitivity, and the number of extra missed level 2 lows. (ii) H2 restated on the curve: the context-over-base rate ratio of compression false alarms at r = 0.95 and 0.99, with a paired patient bootstrap and the H2 reading rule. The pre-registered H2 test stays the δ = 0 result; the curves never replace it.
  - *Check.* The r = 1.00 point must reproduce the v0.1.0 thresholds and test metrics at PISA on every night for every alarm in `rq1_pisa_sweep` and `rq3_kalman`. If it does not, the curves are not reported until the difference is explained.
  - *Expected to change:* no existing number. New outputs: `results/curves/` and `results/tables/curves__*.csv`, a figure, and new text in `results/README.md` that restates the gates and H2 findings on the curves.
- **A12 (2026-10-18; post hoc, after all v0.1.0 results were seen). The upload report no longer lowers time below range (adopts P3, as amended by the project lead).** In simulation a flag overlaps a real low in 37% of runs, so the column "time below range without possible compression artifacts" could leave out real low readings. The report now shows standard time below range only. Possible compression artifacts are shown as labelled annotations on the charts and as a count, next to the flags' measured performance on simulated data in plain words. No number in the report is computed without the flagged readings. PRIVACY_AND_SAFETY.md rule 3 is reworded to match. The flag rule is unchanged. CR12 awaits the clinical co-lead's sign-off before the next app release. *Corrected 2026-10-19:* the A12 app went live before that sign-off, with the Pages deploy of 5f65454 on 2026-10-19; the sign-off is still open. *Expected to change:* the app only; no benchmark number. The flag-survey numbers the app quotes (35% of injected PISAs flagged; a flag on a real low in 37% of runs) are re-run and saved in `results/tables/flag_survey.json`.

- **A13 (2026-10-19; post hoc, after the A11 curves were seen). Gates versus a retuned trend alarm at matched sensitivity.** The A11 curves suggested that making `trend` less eager beats every gate: in the default mix at r = 0.90, `trend` gave 1.06 false alarms/day with 208 of 249 level 2 lows caught, `iforest_causal_gate` 1.29 with 204. Those are point estimates at unmatched sensitivity, with no CI on the difference. A13 tests the claim before anyone makes it. Secondary and exploratory. Definition, fixed before computing:
  - *Question.* At matched held-out level 2 sensitivity, does each comparator give fewer false alarms per day than `trend` with its own prediction threshold swept ("retuned trend")?
  - *Comparators.* `baysal_gate`, `matched_filter_causal_gate`, `iforest_causal_gate`, and the CGM-only `gbm`, which is added to the curves sweep (the A11 curves have only `gbm_time` and `gbm_time_ctx`). All five alarms see the CGM only.
  - *Grid, per mix.* s = retuned trend's held-out level 2 sensitivity at r = 1, minus 0.02, 0.04, 0.06, 0.08 and 0.10 (about 0.80–0.88 default, 0.78–0.86 high, 0.62–0.70 standard).
  - *Resolution.* Within that window the A11 curves have gaps of 1–6 sensitivity points between neighbouring distinct points. For `trend` and the three gates only, r is refined from 0.01 to 0.0025 steps; the choices are recomputed from the stored candidate table, with no re-fit. The remaining granularity is set by D5's candidate thresholds and, for the Baysal gate, by its binary gate, so a finer r cannot add points there. A grid point whose bracketing curve points are more than 0.03 apart in sensitivity is flagged "coarse".
  - *Interpolation and CIs.* In each of 2,000 resamples of the paired, age-stratified patient bootstrap, both pooled curves are recomputed on the same resampled patients (Σ caught / Σ lows; Σ false alarms / Σ days, per r). Points are sorted by sensitivity, ties averaged, and total and compression false alarms per day are interpolated linearly at each s. Linear interpolation between two operating points is attainable by alternating between their thresholds. A draw where either curve does not span s is not estimable. The share of such draws is reported, and a grid point with more than 5% is marked "unstable".
  - *Estimands.* The ratio comparator ÷ retuned trend, for total false alarms per day and for compression false alarms per day.
  - *Reading.* "Adds value" if the upper end of the CI of the total ratio is below 1. "Worse than retuned trend" if the lower end is above 1. Otherwise "no detectable difference".
  - *Scope.* 4 comparators × 5 grid points × 3 mixes, with no multiplicity correction: exploratory.
  - *Limits.* 30 virtual patients and one smooth noise model; thresholds chosen on in-sample training scores (P6).
  - *Expected to change.* No existing number. The curves tables gain `gbm` rows, whose r = 1 point must reproduce v0.1.0. New: `results/tables/curves__a13.csv` and `results/figures/curves__a13.png`.
  - *Result (2026-10-19; `results/tables/curves__a13.csv`).* No published gate gave fewer total false alarms than the retuned trend alarm at any estimable point; the isolation-forest and Baysal gates gave more at most (6 of 8 and 2 of 3). Comparisons are at matched held-out sensitivity (an oracle, not a deployable operating point), cover 18 of 60 planned points, are uncorrected for multiplicity and say nothing about the standard mix. The matched-filter gate could not be compared (its curve reaches only 3 of its 15 planned points, all unstable). CGM-only gradient boosting shifted false alarms between causes: fewer from compression (0.50–0.66 times the retuned trend alarm's, at 7 of its 7 points) with no detectable change in the total (ratios 0.81–1.08, every CI including 1).

## Proposed amendments (not adopted)

Raised by the repository review on 2026-10-18 (`development/REVIEW.md`). P1–P3 were adopted on 2026-10-18 as A10–A12; their original text is kept below. The others are not in effect, and no result reflects them. Each waits for a decision by the project lead; clinical items also need the clinical co-lead.

- **P1 (adopted as A10). Warning-time CIs across patients.** `warning_times` bootstraps patient-runs as if each were a patient, so its CIs are too narrow (BENCHMARK.md §6 requires patients). Proposal: reduce to one value per patient (the median of its runs' medians) before the bootstrap. This changes the warning-time CIs in `warning.csv` only.
- **P2 (adopted as A11, amended). Threshold-free curves.** D5 and BENCHMARK.md §4 require sensitivity–false-alarm curves across thresholds; only operating points were produced. Proposal: on test folds, report level 2 sensitivity against false alarms per day across each alarm's candidate thresholds for the gates and the H2 arms, with patient-bootstrap bands, and false alarms per day at fixed sensitivities of 0.80, 0.85 and 0.90 as an exploratory estimand. Adds outputs; changes no existing number.
- **P3 (adopted as A12, amended). Time below range "without possible compression artifacts" in the upload report.** In simulation a flag overlaps a real low in 37% of runs, so the second column can leave out real lows. Proposal: show time below range once, with the part that falls inside flagged episodes ("4.1%, of which 0.6% inside possible compression artifacts"), and reword PRIVACY_AND_SAFETY.md rule 3 to match. The flag rule is unchanged. Needs CR12.
- **P4. Training horizon in the window arms.** The label horizon is fixed at 30 min, also in `robust_early20` and `robust_early45`, although a code comment says it equals the early window. Proposal: keep it fixed, since those arms test the scoring windows rather than retrained alarms, and correct the comment. The alternative, tying it to the early window, needs a re-run.
- **P5. Treatment rule decoupled from alarm thresholds.** Virtual patients treat at the same cut-offs the alarms use (≤55 mg/dL at night, <70 by day), so true lows end where alarms fire. Proposal: a robustness arm with a different night trigger (for example ≤50 mg/dL, or 15 min of delay). Needs re-simulation and CR17.
- **P6. Out-of-fold scores for threshold choice.** Learned alarms choose θ\* on scores from the data they were fitted on, which is optimistic. The RQ3 comparator is chosen from the same in-sample rates. Proposal: choose thresholds and the RQ3 comparator from inner cross-validation scores on training patients. Changes results.
- **P7. Robustness arms that cover every compared alarm.** The τ-mismatch arms leave out the Kalman alarms, and every robustness arm leaves out the `threshold_combined_*` and `iforest_causal_combined_*` H2 arms. Proposal: add them. Adds rows.
- **P8. Training fault level from training patients.** The realistic PISA fraction is calibrated on the whole default-mix cohort, test patients included. It is a fault-generator setting, not outcome information. Proposal: calibrate it per fold on training patients. May change results slightly.
- **P9. Specified outputs that were never reported.** BENCHMARK.md lists FDA-style alert rates, flicker and a Baysal parameter-set sensitivity analysis. The first two are computed but not reported; the third is never run. Proposal: report them, or remove them from the specification by amendment.
